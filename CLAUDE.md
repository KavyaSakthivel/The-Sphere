# The Sphere — Project & Brand Guidelines

## 1. Project Context

The Sphere is a women-focused wellness and community brand in Coimbatore.

The website supports:
- Brand discovery.
- Membership enquiries.
- Experiences and community storytelling.
- Wellness Carnival information and ticket bookings.

Our intended positioning is a refined, intimate wellness community with the care and hospitality associated with a private members’ club.

These guidelines describe the intended direction. They do not establish unverified facts about the business.

## 2. Audience

Design for affluent and ultra-high-net-worth women, including founders, business leaders, industrialists’ families, and women seeking thoughtful wellness experiences and meaningful connection.

Their priorities may include:
- Discretion and privacy.
- Quality and consistency.
- Personal attention.
- Credible hosts and facilitators.
- Clear information.
- Respect for their time.
- A comfortable sense of belonging.

Do not equate wealth with personal worth or use exclusionary language to manufacture prestige.

## 3. Brand Principles

### Quiet confidence
Communicate value through specific details, thoughtful design, and credible experiences.

### Warm hospitality
The website should feel like a considered personal introduction.

### Intimacy with clarity
A selective atmosphere must still explain what The Sphere offers and how to enquire.

### Trust before persuasion
Accurate information and reliable interactions matter more than promotional claims.

### Restraint
Every section, image, animation, and call to action should serve a clear purpose.

## 4. Business Goals

Primary:
- Generate qualified membership enquiries.

Secondary:
- Explain the membership experience.
- Establish founder and programme credibility.
- Communicate the brand’s relationship with Coimbatore.
- Support relevant experiences and Carnival bookings.
- Help visitors understand the next step.

Do not optimise solely for submission volume or ticket sales at the expense of brand coherence.

## 5. Facts and Claims

Never invent or imply:
- Membership prices.
- Invitation-only access.
- Application screening or acceptance criteria.
- A permanent clubhouse or owned venue.
- Member counts.
- Testimonials.
- Partnerships.
- Facilitator credentials.
- Medical or wellness outcomes.
- Response times.
- Privacy guarantees.
- Scarcity or limited availability.

Verify claims against approved content or ask for confirmation.

Distinguish existing business facts from proposed positioning.

Do not publish placeholder contact details or change email links to an address that has not been set up.

## 6. Voice and Editorial Style

Write with:
- Warmth.
- Precision.
- Confidence.
- Simplicity.
- Understatement.

Use consistent British/Indian English.

Prefer concrete details about experiences, people, places, and membership over abstract promises.

Avoid:
- Aggressive sales language.
- Manufactured urgency.
- Excessive adjectives.
- Repeated use of “exclusive,” “elite,” or “discerning.”
- Generic phrases such as “unlock your potential” or “elevate your journey.”
- Unsupported transformation claims.
- Pretentious or ornamental language.
- Copying another brand’s distinctive wording.

Luxury references such as Aman or Soho House are references for restraint and hospitality, not templates to imitate.

### Copy checks
Before approving copy, check:
- Grammar and singular/plural agreement.
- Consistent terminology.
- Clear meaning.
- Repetition across sections.
- Factual accuracy.
- Whether the visitor learns something useful.

## 7. Visual Direction

Aim for:
- Editorial typography.
- Generous, purposeful whitespace.
- Calm section rhythm.
- Restrained colour.
- Consistent image treatment.
- Authentic photography.
- Clear hierarchy.
- Subtle motion.
- Carefully considered mobile layouts.

Preserve the existing brand identity unless a redesign is explicitly requested.

Do not assume premium design requires:
- Tiny text.
- Pale, unreadable colours.
- Excessive empty space.
- Hidden navigation.
- Slow transitions.
- Decorative cursive typography.
- Gold accents.
- Large videos that harm usability.

Use existing design tokens and components where practical.

Recommend specific adjustments rather than saying “make it more premium.”

## 8. Information Architecture

Visitors should quickly understand:
1. What The Sphere is.
2. Who it is for.
3. What members experience.
4. Where experiences take place.
5. How membership works.
6. What happens after an enquiry.

Each page should have a clear purpose and a primary next step.

Avoid repeating the same message across multiple sections without adding information.

### Core content
Where applicable, explain:
- Move.
- Pause.
- Connect.
- Discover.
- The six-month membership rhythm.

Describe actual experiences and cadence using confirmed information.

## 9. Membership and Wellness Carnival

Membership and the Carnival should feel connected within one brand.

Evaluate their prominence against current business priorities.

Do not let event promotions unintentionally obscure the main brand introduction.

Event announcements should:
- Be easy to dismiss.
- Remain usable on mobile.
- Avoid unnecessary repetition.
- Explain the relationship to The Sphere.
- Be removed or updated when the event ends.

Preserve clear event pricing, venue details, schedules, terms, and booking access.

Do not hide essential event information to create an appearance of exclusivity.

## 10. Membership Enquiry Flow

The enquiry experience should feel personal, clear, and considered.

Use the lightest accurate description:
- Enquiry.
- Expression of interest.
- Application, only if there is a real application process.

Collect only information needed for the next step.

Do not add questions about income, family status, or social standing to manufacture selectivity.

Forms should include:
- Persistent labels.
- Appropriate input types.
- Useful autocomplete.
- Clear required and optional fields.
- Accessible validation.
- Loading feedback.
- Duplicate-submission protection where applicable.
- Clear success and error messages.
- Relevant consent and privacy information.

Explain next steps using confirmed operational details.

Never promise acceptance or a response deadline without approval.

## 11. Trust and Privacy

Build trust through verified information about:
- Founders.
- Hosts and facilitators.
- Experiences.
- Venues.
- Contact channels.
- Relevant policies.

Protect member discretion.

Do not publish names, photographs, affiliations, testimonials, or personal stories without appropriate permission.

Do not claim confidentiality practices that have not been established.

Avoid placing personal data, credentials, API keys, or payment information in frontend code, logs, screenshots, or documentation.

## 12. Accessibility and Mobile Quality

Accessibility is part of hospitality.

Check:
- Readable type.
- Sufficient contrast.
- Logical heading structure.
- Keyboard navigation.
- Visible focus.
- Accessible control names.
- Proper form labels.
- Meaningful alternative text.
- Reduced-motion preferences.
- Comfortable touch targets.
- No horizontal overflow.
- No sticky elements obscuring content.
- Usable modals and mobile navigation.
- Correct focus management and background scroll handling.
- On-screen keyboard compatibility.

Never sacrifice essential usability for aesthetics.

## 13. Performance and Technical Quality

Keep the experience responsive and dependable.

Prioritise:
- Appropriately sized images.
- Efficient video delivery.
- Stable layouts.
- Sensible font loading.
- Minimal unnecessary scripts.
- Working links and assets.
- Useful page titles and descriptions.
- Correct social sharing metadata.

Report measured performance separately from visual impressions.

Do not invent Lighthouse scores, loading times, or Core Web Vitals results.

Follow the existing project stack and conventions. Avoid introducing dependencies without a clear need.

## 14. Existing Integrations

Treat booking, payments, enquiries, consent, and analytics as functional product systems.

Before editing related code:
- Trace the existing flow.
- Identify affected services and configuration.
- Understand validation and failure handling.
- Preserve verified payment confirmation.
- Preserve duplicate protection and reconciliation logic.
- Preserve consent requirements.
- Keep secrets server-side.

Do not simplify payment verification or record a booking as paid based solely on a browser redirect.

Avoid unrelated changes while implementing visual or copy improvements.

## 15. Audit Standards

Base findings on actual inspection.

For significant issues, record:
- Page or route.
- Component or section.
- Observed behaviour or exact copy.
- Relevant viewport.
- Why it matters.
- A concrete recommendation.

Label evidence as:
- Observed in browser.
- Confirmed in source.
- Inferred.
- Requires confirmation.

State inspection limitations.

Do not claim to have tested pages, links, devices, or interactions that were not checked.

### Priorities
- P0: Broken essential journey, serious accessibility barrier, or material trust issue.
- P1: Major positioning, clarity, or enquiry improvement.
- P2: Meaningful content or design refinement.
- P3: Optional enhancement.

Assess brand impact, usability impact, effort, and dependencies separately.

## 16. Working Rules

### Audit requests
Inspect and recommend. Do not change code unless implementation is requested.

### Implementation requests
Make the requested changes locally within the authorised scope.

Before editing:
- Read relevant project instructions.
- Inspect affected code.
- Understand existing behaviour.
- Identify dependencies.

During implementation:
- Prefer focused, reversible changes.
- Reuse existing components.
- Preserve unrelated work.
- Avoid unnecessary rewrites.
- Flag factual dependencies instead of inventing answers.

After implementation:
- Run checks appropriate to the change.
- Inspect desktop and mobile layouts where possible.
- Summarise what changed.
- Show the diff.
- Provide matching before-and-after screenshots when available.
- State remaining limitations honestly.

Do not commit, push, deploy, publish, submit live forms, initiate payments, or change live service settings unless explicitly requested.

Respect the permission settings of the development environment.
