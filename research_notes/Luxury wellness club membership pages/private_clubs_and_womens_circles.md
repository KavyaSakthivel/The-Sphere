## 1. Overall look: palette, typography, photography, whitespace, mood (per site)

### Takeaway
Almost every site in this set is built from the same three ingredients: a warm off-white ground (never pure white), one deep "club" colour (bottle green, oxblood, aubergine, espresso), and a single small accent used only on the call to action; headings are a classical serif and everything functional is a small sans, often in tracked capitals. The more exclusive the club, the less is on the page: 5 Hertford Street, Zero Bond and Annabel's show little more than a wordmark.

### Inferences
- The palette formula that recurs (warm off-white + one deep colour + one accent reserved for the button) is cheap to reproduce and is what makes a page read as "club" rather than "studio" or "spa". Bottle green with cream is the most common pairing in this set (Conduit, Quorum, Chief, Casa Cipriani footer, Ned).
- The medical retreats (SHA, Lanserhof, Chenot) use cool greys and whites and read clinical; the hospitality-led ones (Kamalaya, Aman) use warm sand. For a women's circle the warm register is the relevant one.
- A club can look expensive with no interior photography at all (Maison Estelle, 5 Hertford Street, Zero Bond rely entirely on type, colour and a crest). This suits a circle whose venue is shared privately.
- The two sites nearest in spirit to a small women's circle (leap.club, Circle Burbank) are both visibly home-made (Canva-style and Wix builds). Polish is not what signals intimacy on those pages; candid group photos and a personal voice do.

## 2. Membership / "join" page: section order, length and amount of copy

### Takeaway
Membership pages are short. The community-led ones run 600 to 750 words in total and follow one order: a one-line promise, what you get, proof from members, the price, then how joining works, with the call to action repeated two or three times. The grand clubs say far less (100 to 250 words) and push everything else behind an application.

### Cited Findings
- The Circle Burbank, top to bottom: (1) "Membership" heading with the price directly under it as a second heading; (2) centred list of eight inclusions; (3) "Apply Here" button; (4) "Eligibility" block (four bullet points); (5) "Our Values & House Rules" (eight short numbered rules, including confidentiality and a judgment-free rule); (6) "Application Process" (one paragraph, then Step 1 to Step 4, with the button repeated beside Step 1); (7) hours and address; (8) newsletter. About 616 words, page height about 3,100px at 1280 wide. — [thecircleburbank.com/membership](https://www.thecircleburbank.com/membership)
- leap.club 2.0, top to bottom: (1) hero with two-line headline and a two-line description of who it is for; (2) "request an invite" button; (3) strip of three member group photos; (4) "different rooms. different kinds of magic." with five named gathering formats, each one sentence; (5) "how this works" as three columns under pill labels (the waitlist, the shortlist, the curation); (6) city line; (7) closing statement, "know more" link, "join the waitlist" button; (8) three social links. Scroll height about 3,600px; roughly 250 words. No navigation menu at all. — [leap.club](https://leap.club/)
- The Trouble Club join page: (1) "Become a member" headline and one-line description; (2) "In the last 12 months our members have..." - Kamalaya programme page (for contrast): about 1,100 words and 7,600px, including a full price and inclusions table, treatment explanations and policies. — [kamalaya.com/radiant-bliss-program](https://kamalaya.com/radiant-bliss-program/)

### Inferences
- A workable order for a single-offer circle, taken from the Circle Burbank, leap.club and Trouble pages: promise and who it is for; what a membership includes; a few photographs and member voices; the fee, stated once and plainly; how joining works in three or four steps; one closing call to action. Around 300 to 600 words is the norm for the closest analogues.
- Describing membership by rhythm ("Weekly", "Monthly", "Across the year" on The Conduit) or by named gathering formats (leap.club's five "rooms") tells a reader what her months will look like better than a benefits list does. A circle with one gathering a month can present the six gatherings the same way.
- The Trouble Club's "In the last 12 months our members have..." device (a list of specific past evenings) is the most concrete substitute for a benefits list found in this set and needs no property or budget, only real past events.
- Values or house rules placed on the membership page itself (Circle Burbank) do the work of explaining who the club suits without an eligibility test.

### Gaps
- The actual application forms behind "Apply" buttons (Soho House, Chief, Conduit, WIE, Annabel's, leap.club's Typeform) were not opened or filled, so their length and questions are not verified, except Jolie's, whose first form step is visible on the page.
- Casa Cipriani's New York application page (members.casacipriani.com/application) was not opened.

## 3. How price is handled; single offer versus tiers

### Takeaway
There are three behaviours: small community-led clubs state the price openly and early; mid-size clubs state it late, beside the apply button, or behind a "calculate pricing" step; the most exclusive show none. Where there is one offer, the price is set as a heading in its own right with no card or table.

### Cited Findings
- Open, single offer, at the very top: The Circle Burbank prints "$250 per month" as the second line of the page, in the same display face as the title, above the inclusions list. No tiers. — [thecircleburbank.com/membership](https://www.thecircleburbank.com/membership)
- Open, tiered: The Trouble Club shows three cards (Digital £6, Core £19, Unlimited £49 per month) with a monthly/annual toggle, "Free 30-day trial" tags on two cards and a "Join Now" button under each. It adds a note that anyone who cannot afford the fee can email for a discounted membership. — [thetroubleclub.com/become-a-member](https://thetroubleclub.com/become-a-member)
- Open, two options, placed late: The Conduit shows "£1,500 per year" and "£1,250 per year" (not-for-profit) as small capital labels above each option name, only in the sixth section, each with its own apply button; its FAQ repeats the figures and adds that a monthly payment plan exists and that memberships are 12-month contracts. — [theconduit.com/membership](https://www.theconduit.com/membership/), [theconduit.com/membership-faqs](https://www.theconduit.com/membership-faqs/)
- Open, with tax stated: Casa Cipriani Milano lists four memberships in a row, each with "Annual fee", "One-time Initiation fee" and "(Excluding VAT)" on its own line, and a plain outlined "Apply" button. - "From" pricing in running text: Soho House Mumbai states in a sentence under the description that membership "starts from ₹45,500 quarterly" with the Under 27 figure in brackets, directly above the apply button. - Flat single figure in a sentence: Soho Friends is given as one line with three currencies ("£135/ $175/ €155 per year"). — [sohohouse.com/en-us/membership/soho-friends](https://www.sohohouse.com/en-us/membership/soho-friends)

The "Woman Only" category is for the primary member alone with no partner add-on. — [thequorumclub.com/join-the-q](https://thequorumclub.com/join-the-q/)
- AllBright everywoman lists individual prices in a "Price:" line at the end of each block, with tax stated inline ("£350 (inc vat) per annum"; SME bundles "+VAT"). — [allbrighteverywoman.com/memberships](https://allbrighteverywoman.com/memberships/)
- Retreats: Kamalaya publishes a full table ("Package price (THB) (excluding accommodation)" for 8, 15 and 21 nights) with a validity date; Chenot shows "CHF" figures against programmes on its home page; SHA explains a fixed nightly rate that includes room, board and programme and says taxes are included; Atmantan shows durations ("Starts from" a number of nights) and no rupee prices, routing price questions to a call-back or WhatsApp. — [kamalaya.com/radiant-bliss-program](https://kamalaya.com/radiant-bliss-program/), [chenotpalaceweggis.com/en](https://www.chenotpalaceweggis.com/en/), [shawellness.com/en/programs-packs](https://shawellness.com/en/programs-packs/), [atmantan.com/wellness-packages/atmantan-living](https://www.atmantan.com/wellness-packages/atmantan-living/)

### Inferences
- For a single ₹10,000 six-month offer the closest precedent is Circle Burbank: the price as a heading, immediately followed by what it includes. The Soho House Mumbai sentence form is the quieter alternative when the price should not dominate.
- Every site that shows a price also states the period right beside it ("per month", "per year", "quarterly") and, where tax applies, the tax position on the same or next line ("Excluding VAT", "inc vat"). The Indian equivalent is to state the GST position next to the figure.
- Hidden pricing correlates with a vetting step and a sales conversation. A circle that takes payment by a link has already chosen the open model; hiding the price would contradict the payment button.
- The Trouble Club hardship line shows a way to state a firm price while sounding generous rather than exclusionary.

### Gaps
- No fee for Chief, WIE Suite, leap.club, Jolie's or The Quorum could be verified on their own sites. Press figures for The Quorum (joining fee ₹3 to 5 lakh, annual ₹1 to 2 lakh, per [The Core](https://www.thecore.in/podcasts/inside-indias-booming-private-members-only-club-scene-837007)) and Jolie's (from ₹3 lakh for one year for under-27s in 2022, per [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/magazine/back-of-the-book/story/the-rise-of-members-only-clubs-for-millennials-345840-2022-08-29)) come from search summaries of those articles, not from pages read in full.
- Whether leap.club charges per gathering or by membership is not stated on its page.

## 4. Primary call to action: exact wording, styling and position

### Takeaway
The dominant verb is "Apply" (for clubs that vet) and "Join" (for clubs that take payment directly); invitation-led Indian sites use "Request an invite" and "Join the waitlist". The button is small, usually a pill or a thin outlined rectangle, appears in the first screen and again at the very end, and is the only element allowed to use the accent colour.

### Cited Findings
- Soho House: "Apply for membership" under the H1 and again at the end; black pill, white 14px bold sans, radius 40px (CSS). Inside the application panel the wording changes to "Start application" (light pill) with "Calculate pricing" as an outlined secondary. — [sohohouse.com/en-us/membership](https://www.sohohouse.com/en-us/membership)
- Chief: "Apply" in the header on every page and in the hero; lime pill (CSS background rgb(215,243,49), text rgb(17,30,4), radius 999px, 13 to 15px medium sans). — [chief.com/membership](https://chief.com/membership)

## 5. Communicating intimacy, privacy, "limited numbers" and "the right members" without sounding salesy or exclusionary

### Takeaway
The persuasive sites describe the room and the people in it, state the size as a plain fact, and frame selection as care for the group ("fit", "chemistry", "values") instead of status. Scarcity is said once, calmly, and never with countdowns or "only X left" devices; the only discount pop-up in the whole set (Trouble Club) is also the least club-like moment.

### Cited Findings
- leap.club frames curation as attention to the mix of people: it says applications are read individually and that it looks past titles to interests, life stage and "room chemistry", and ends with "the product is the room." Small size is stated in the hero description of the gatherings. — [leap.club](https://leap.club/)
- The Circle Burbank says it has a limited number of memberships and that this keeps the space a sanctuary that is safe, intimate and "rooted in trust"; eligibility is expressed as values (inclusivity, respect, confidentiality, willingness to take part), and confidentiality is house rule two. The home page says applications are open and space is limited. — [thecircleburbank.com/membership](https://www.thecircleburbank.com/membership), [thecircleburbank.com](https://www.thecircleburbank.com/)
- Chief describes its Core Groups as "small, confidential" peer groups, and separately publishes a blunt criteria page including a "not for those who" list. — [chief.com/membership](https://chief.com/membership), [chief.com/membership-criteria](https://chief.com/membership-criteria)
- The WIE Suite quotes a member on "immediate intimacy and trust" and states cohort size as a number (eight-person groups meeting monthly); its criteria are corporate-seniority lists. — [thewiesuite.com](https://www.thewiesuite.com/), [thewiesuite.com/membership](https://www.thewiesuite.com/membership)
- The Conduit heads its joining section "Membership is by application", says what qualities it looks for (impact, insight, influence, integrity) and asks applicants to say who they are and what matters to them; it also says "This isn't a members' club" to distance itself from exclusivity. — [theconduit.com/membership](https://www.theconduit.com/membership/)
- Maison Estelle describes its community as people with "plenty to say and nothing to prove" and says nothing else about selection. — [maisonestelle.com](https://maisonestelle.com/)
- The Quorum opens its join page with a single line that owns privacy lightly ("We are private, but the best things are, aren't they?") and on its home page calls itself both inclusive and exclusive. — [thequorumclub.com/join-the-q](https://thequorumclub.com/join-the-q/), [thequorumclub.com](https://thequorumclub.com/)
- Jolie's uses a playful page title about the door being "ever so slightly ajar", and states the rule plainly: entry by invite or referral plus the committee's approval. — [clubjolies.com/pages/membership](https://clubjolies.com/pages/membership)
- Casa Cipriani describes its community as eclectic, culturally curious and international, across industries, countries and age groups, i.e. it defines "right members" by curiosity and mix. — [casacipriani.com](https://www.casacipriani.com/)
- The Trouble Club is the counter-example: explicitly inclusive ("mainly women", hardship pricing, 30-day free trial), with a newsletter discount pop-up over the join page. — [thetroubleclub.com/become-a-member](https://thetroubleclub.com/become-a-member)
- 5 Hertford Street, Zero Bond and The Twenty Two communicate privacy by absence: no description, no photographs of members, a login link. — [5hertfordstreet.com](https://www.5hertfordstreet.com/), [zerobondny.com](https://zerobondny.com/), [london.thetwentytwo.com](https://london.thetwentytwo.com/)
- Indian press describes the current mood in new Indian clubs as a move from spectacle towards ease, privacy and belonging, with small personal gatherings. — [Harper's Bazaar India, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.harpersbazaar.in/culture/story/how-indias-creative-class-is-reimagining-the-private-club-1358609-2026-03-12)

### Inferences
- The gentlest working formula, used by the two nearest analogues, has three parts: state the size as a fact; give the reason in terms of what members gain (trust, depth of conversation); describe who thrives there by temperament or values. Seniority criteria (Chief, WIE) are the part that reads as exclusionary and is not needed for a wellness circle.
- Confidentiality promises ("what is shared stays here") are a women-led pattern and double as the privacy message; the grand clubs express privacy through no-photo policies and empty websites instead.
- Withholding the venue fits this vocabulary: several clubs show no interiors at all, and leap.club sends "the details" only with the invitation. Saying that the address is shared with members after joining reads as discretion, in line with these sites.
- Urgency devices (discount pop-ups, trial tags) appear only on the high-volume, low-price club and visibly cheapen the page.

### Gaps
- No site visited states an actual member cap as a number on its membership page; "limited" is always unquantified. Whether stating a number helps is untested here.
- House rules on phones and photography (a standard privacy signal at Soho House and Birley clubs) were not found on the pages visited; the Soho House Mumbai "important information" page returned no relevant text.

## 6. How community-led clubs show people

### Takeaway
Community-led clubs show members together, in candid group photographs at tables, and let named members speak in short quotes; property-led clubs show empty rooms. No site visited uses a founder letter, handwriting or a signature on its membership page, so that device is open territory.

### Cited Findings
- leap.club: a strip of three informal group photographs (women around restaurant tables, one long outdoor table shot from above, a group on a staircase) directly under the first button; no captions, no names, no founder. — [leap.club](https://leap.club/)

- The Trouble Club: four one-line anonymous member quotes laid diagonally over a darkened crowd photograph; a portrait of a laughing speaker beside the details section; the About page shows the chief executive and events manager with portraits and third-person bios, and no founder letter. — [thetroubleclub.com/become-a-member](https://thetroubleclub.com/become-a-member), [thetroubleclub.com/about](https://thetroubleclub.com/about)
- The Circle Burbank: founder shown on a separate Team page with a portrait and a third-person paragraph under "Meet the Founder"; no letter or signature. The joining steps do personalise her role: step three is a conversation with the founder. — [thecircleburbank.com/team](https://www.thecircleburbank.com/team), [thecircleburbank.com/membership](https://www.thecircleburbank.com/membership)
- The Coven: three named member quotes with roles in a carousel on the home page. — [thecoven.com](https://www.thecoven.com/)
- Chief: candid event photograph of members in conversation; no named individuals on the membership page. — [chief.com/membership](https://chief.com/membership)

- Casa Cipriani: opens with a quotation attributed to Arrigo Cipriani and tells the family story across four generations; the only founder-voice device found on a club site. — [casacipriani.com](https://www.casacipriani.com/)
- Soho House, Ned's Club, Jolie's, Maison Estelle: spaces, food and atmosphere; members appear only as crowd in event photos (Soho) or not at all. — [sohohouse.com/en-us/membership](https://www.sohohouse.com/en-us/membership), [thened.com/london/membership/club](https://www.thened.com/london/membership/club), [clubjolies.com](https://clubjolies.com/), [maisonestelle.com](https://maisonestelle.com/)

### Inferences
- The most transferable "people" pattern for a small circle is leap.club's: three honest group photographs high on the page, before any explanation. It shows size, age range, dress and mood at a glance.

- Faces at a table communicate intimacy more directly than any interior; this matters when the venue is not shown.

### Gaps
- Hand-written or signature touches: none observed on any page visited. Press coverage of such devices was not found in the searches run.
- leap.club's "know more" link destination (probably its Substack) was not opened, so any founder voice there is unverified.

## 7. How the joining process is explained

### Takeaway
The clearest pages reduce joining to three or four short numbered or labelled steps placed just above the final call to action; the grand clubs explain nothing publicly. Good pages also say what happens immediately after the click.

### Cited Findings
- The Circle Burbank: one framing paragraph, then "Step 1" to "Step 4" as bold labels with one line each: fill out the application; it is reviewed; a scheduled video conversation with the founder; an email with a link to join or a place on the wait list. — [thecircleburbank.com/membership](https://www.thecircleburbank.com/membership)
- leap.club: "how this works" as three columns, each with a pill label (the waitlist, the shortlist, the curation), a bold five-word summary and two sentences: tell us about yourself; we read every application; get an invitation when there is a fit in your city. A map-pin line with the three cities follows. — [leap.club](https://leap.club/)
- The Trouble Club: one paragraph under "A few details": choose a plan, complete a short form, then sign up and book events immediately; renewal and cancellation terms in the next paragraph. — [thetroubleclub.com/become-a-member](https://thetroubleclub.com/become-a-member)
- Soho House: a panel stating the application takes about 15 minutes, can be saved, and listing three things needed (a recent photo, card details, information about interests and occupation). — [sohohouse.com/en-us/membership](https://www.sohohouse.com/en-us/membership)
- The Conduit: no steps; instead two human routes before applying (an in-person meeting with a tour, or a call) with hours of availability, plus a full FAQ page. — [theconduit.com/membership](https://www.theconduit.com/membership/), [theconduit.com/membership-faqs](https://www.theconduit.com/membership-faqs/)
- Chief: process handled entirely by a categorised FAQ (Membership, Applications, Core, Coaching, Events, Community, Clubhouses) with questions such as what to expect from the application process; criteria on a separate page. — [chief.com/faq](https://chief.com/faq)
- Jolie's: two sentences (waitlist, then invite or referral and committee approval) and a two-step form asking for name, phone, email, age, gender, company, a social handle, a few lines about yourself and the name of a member you know. — [clubjolies.com/pages/membership](https://clubjolies.com/pages/membership)
- The Quorum: a pop-up form asking preferred club, how you heard about them and a reference to an existing member (with an "I don't know a member" option), followed by a message that a membership representative will make contact. — [thequorumclub.com/join-the-q](https://thequorumclub.com/join-the-q/)
- Maison Estelle, Annabel's, 5 Hertford Street, Zero Bond: no public explanation. — [maisonestelle.com](https://maisonestelle.com/), [annabels.co.uk](https://annabels.co.uk/), [5hertfordstreet.com](https://www.5hertfordstreet.com/), [zerobondny.com](https://zerobondny.com/)

### Inferences
- Three steps, each a bold phrase plus one or two sentences, is the format used by the nearest Indian analogue and fits a pay-by-link flow naturally (pay; receive confirmation; receive the venue and date privately).
- Stating what follows payment or application (Trouble: book immediately; Quorum: a representative will contact you; Soho: 15 minutes) reduces hesitation at the button. For a private-venue circle the equivalent reassurance is when and how the address arrives.
- An FAQ is the format of large organisations with many edge cases (Chief, Conduit). The small clubs fold the two or three real questions (renewal, cancellation, what happens next) into a short "a few details" paragraph.

### Gaps
- Timelines (how long review takes) are not stated on any page visited.
- What applicants actually receive after payment or approval (welcome email, WhatsApp message) could not be observed without submitting forms.

## 8. What a small club can borrow versus what only works with a big property and budget

### Takeaway
Type, colour, restraint, a plainly stated price, three numbered steps, candid group photos and specific descriptions of past gatherings are all free to copy. Amenity lists, big numbers, galleries of interiors, tier matrices, reciprocal networks and application portals depend on property and scale.

- Depends on property and scale: Soho House's headline numbers (48 Houses, 22 gyms, 2,400 monthly events) and photo carousels; Ned's Club's fourteen blocks on rooftop, vault bar, gym and spa; Jolie's nine named zones over 30,000 square feet; The Quorum's reciprocal clubs; Chief's four clubhouses and partner-brand logo wall; The Conduit's in-person tours; Casa Cipriani's 1931 heritage story. — [sohohouse.com/en-us/membership](https://www.sohohouse.com/en-us/membership), [thened.com/london/membership/club](https://www.thened.com/london/membership/club), [clubjolies.com](https://clubjolies.com/), [thequorumclub.com](https://thequorumclub.com/), [chief.com](https://chief.com/), [theconduit.com/membership](https://www.theconduit.com/membership/), [casacipriani.com](https://www.casacipriani.com/)

### Inferences
- A page that shows ₹10,000 for six months with a direct payment button is more open than any Indian club page found. The nearest precedents for that openness are foreign (Circle Burbank, Trouble Club). It is a point of difference, and the surrounding page has to carry the "private" feeling through tone, restraint and the privately shared venue.
- Because no Indian site in the set states its GST position, there is no local convention to copy from these pages; the European convention is a short bracketed line directly under or beside the figure. "Inclusive of GST" in that position would follow the same logic.
- A search result title for a hotel booking site mentions a Soho Friends membership being added "for 11800 INR" ([Mr & Mrs Smith listing](https://www.mrandmrssmith.com/luxury-hotels/soho-house-mumbai/offers/68803/68803-early-booking-soho-friends-rate-if-youre-not-a-soho-house-member-a-12-month-soho-friends-membership-will-be-added-to-your-booking-for-11800-inr-see-tandcs)); ₹11,800 equals ₹10,000 plus 18%, which suggests GST-inclusive consumer pricing in that case. - WhatsApp is the expected human channel in India and is treated as legitimate even by luxury operators (Atmantan's "Official WhatsApp", SHA's WhatsApp links). A single named WhatsApp contact beside the payment button would match what this audience sees elsewhere; three phone numbers in a footer (Jolie's) is the less elegant version of the same instinct.
