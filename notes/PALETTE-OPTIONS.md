# Sphere palette study — 8 October 2026

The client requested several polished alternatives, compatible with the supplied
logo and quieter than the rejected multicolor scheme. These are design proposals;
the current local site retains its evergreen direction while the alternatives
are compared. The logo artwork remains unchanged. Every option keeps original
ivory and uses one deep primary color, one quiet secondary surface and a small
brand accent. Accent colors are not used for body text or full-page section fills.

## Intended brand impressions

- Forest Atelier: grounded, discreet and calm. The closest extension of the
  original sage mark; a strong option for a private wellness club. Its much deeper
  value supplies contrast that the pale branding colors alone cannot provide.
- Ink & Pearl: composed, contemporary and assured. The cooler dark blue feels
  more formal; warm ivory prevents a clinical reading. Best for a more editorial,
  professional identity rather than an earthy retreat identity.
- Aubergine & Oat: intimate, distinctive and softly opulent. A low-saturation
  plum is a possible boutique direction without pink-heavy styling. Keep the sage
  mark small and let ivory separate it from plum rather than alternating color blocks.
- Espresso & Linen: warm, tactile and established. Brown-black connects visually
  to the supplied bamboo, timber and bronze-bowl footage. The sage mark is the
  botanical counterpoint. This is the strongest warmer alternative.

Forest is the closest brand fit; Espresso is the strongest alternative if the
client wants a noticeably warmer look. Richness comes from deep/light contrast,
consistent color hierarchy and material photography. It does not require shiny
gold, multiple accent families or a different section color on every scroll.
Keep most reading surfaces in ivory and stone; concentrate the deep color in
navigation, headings, invitations, footer and primary actions. Use accent only
in the supplied mark and small details. Compact, intentional section spacing
should support the editorial rhythm instead of filling empty areas with color.

## Evidence and limits

These descriptions are design interpretations for this brand, not universal
psychological effects or claims about all affluent audiences. Marketing research
finds that hue and saturation/value can contribute to perceived brand personality:
Labrecque & Milne (2012), [Exciting red and competent blue](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-010-0245-y).
Elliot's [2015 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00368/full)
emphasizes context and caution in applying color psychology. Here the logo,
women's community, actual photography and typography provide the relevant context.

The interactive study presents all four palettes on the same restrained layout
so differences in layout do not bias the color comparison. It uses the actual
ivory wordmark and supplied gathering photograph. The preview is responsive at
320px and 736px; each selection updates the surfaces, text, swatches and hex codes.
